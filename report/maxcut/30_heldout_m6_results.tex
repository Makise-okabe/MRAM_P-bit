\section{Independent sparse held-out validation (M6)}
\label{sec:m6-heldout}

We next evaluated the pre-frozen sparse Biq Mac weighted Max-Cut
instance \texttt{t2g10\_7777} with 100 spins, 200 original
nonzero source edges and the published cut optimum $6{,}509{,}837$.
Prior to accessing that source graph, the isolated M6 runner passed
synthetic and \texttt{t2g10\_5555} CAL smoke tests and wrote a
parameter- and source-hash-frozen protocol. The on-host
pre-execution protocol equality check passed. The source gate found
100 vertices, 200 raw entries, 200 nonzero edges and zero zero-weight
edges. Exact source file hashes are preserved in the experimental
host's manifest and run-level CSV archive.

We preserved the M5 comparison: one common persistent
\emph{synthetic} device-offset population at $d\in\{0,0.8,1.6\}$
logit, unit gain, 24 paired solver seeds, and 50,000 attempted
asynchronous Gibbs updates per condition. The formats are reference
FP64, six-bit E3M2 FP6, and signed INT6, with the same source-only
global-scale codebooks. All $3\times3\times24=216$ runs completed.
The subsequent local archive audit verified the source-data and
frozen-protocol hashes, all 216 unique records, nine format--offset
quality rows, six contrast rows, 144 static snapshot rows, six
snapshot summaries and six generated figures. No solver reruns were
needed for figure generation.

\subsection{Predeclared static probability-fidelity test}
The frozen prediction expected FP6 to show \emph{lower} mean
absolute probability distortion than INT6 at $d=0.8$ and
$d=1.6$ on common returned FP64 reference states. The M6
results \emph{contradict this directional prediction}.
The paired quantity below is
\[
D_P=\langle|\Delta P|\rangle_{\mathrm{FP6}}
-\langle|\Delta P|\rangle_{\mathrm{INT6}},
\]
where each $\Delta P$ is an exact sigmoid-probability difference
computed at identical spins, offset population and beta.
Positive $D_P$ favours INT6; negative favours FP6.

\subsection{Meaning of the contrasting dense and sparse findings}
M5 and M6 used the same frozen experimental procedure but
different graph instances. The \emph{direction} of the
static probability-fidelity ranking reversed: FP6 was
favoured on the dense graph at all three offsets, whereas
INT6 is favoured on the sparse graph with paired CI support.
This finding is graph-conditioned; differences in topology,
weight distribution and the solver's visited states are
not isolated experimentally, so sparsity alone cannot be
assigned causal responsibility.

Moreover, lower static response distortion is insufficient
to predict certification of finite-budget optimization quality.
At sparse $d=1.6$, INT6 has better static fidelity, but FP6
passes the two frozen quality screens while INT6 does not.
The direct FP6-minus-INT6 solver gap CI still crosses zero.
The archived M6 paired_contrasts.csv now verifies the within-graph
offset difference-in-differences: at $d=0.8$,
$-1.167$ pp, 95\% CI $[-2.791,+0.468]$, and at $d=1.6$,
$-0.845$ pp, CI $[-2.215,+0.556]$. Both intervals span
zero. A formal pooled graph-by-format interaction has not
been estimated. Further field-error and hardware-cost
interpretation is presented in Section~\ref{sec:heldout-synthesis}.

\noindent\textbf{Boundary and provenance.}
Both held-out graphs are now completed, but only two
distinct source instances and one fixed \emph{assumed},
not measured, device population have been studied.
The mean original-cut gaps were computed using the original
signed weights, while the solver retains the earliest best
\emph{represented}-energy state. The M6 raw files and six generated figures passed an on-host
consistency audit and are now archived on the
\href{https://github.com/Makise-okabe/MRAM_P-bit/tree/research/m4-gain-fusion-20261107/results/m2_heldout_sparse_fp6_int6_20261110}
{research branch}. The archived files include the original 216
runs, frozen protocol and graph hashes, complete quality/contrast
tables, static snapshots and six PNG/PDF figures.
No new solver evaluations were made during this report update.
