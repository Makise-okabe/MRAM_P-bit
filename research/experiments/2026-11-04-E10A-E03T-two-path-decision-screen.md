## E10A — Directional Ising operator/correction (synthetic)

- Target: symmetric weighted Max-Cut couplings `J=-W`. Assumed physical error model for *existing edges only*: `H_ij=J_ij+e_sym+e_skew`, `H_ji=J_ji+e_sym-e_skew`, with per-chip fixed, Gaussian components `sigma_sym=.15`, `sigma_skew=.35` in abstract coupling units. No actual directional error observed in the GF MRAM macro; user-owned MTJ versus foundry native cell remains unresolved.
- Six independently generated 12–16-spin weighted random graphs, six chip assignments per graph, 40 restarts per chip, eight sweeps, exact Max-Cut optima by enumeration; all six arms paired on chip and external random stream. `1440` restarts per policy, **36 graph/chip clusters**.
- Methods: exact digital incremental, raw directed, full pair symmetrization, random 25%-edge correction, 25% selected by antisymmetric mismatch magnitude, and 25% selected by antisymmetric mismatch *independent calibration spin flip exposure*. All partial-correction policies get the *same full 2|E| directed coefficient readings* as the full-sym comparator; correction-count alone is NOT a fair sensing-cost comparison. Flip-exposure policy additionally pays an extra calibration trajectory. Real physical cost model pending.
- Best-visited exact optimum hits /1440: digital 1035 (71.875%), raw 963 (66.875%), full sym 1017 (70.625%), magnitude 977 (67.847%), flip exposure 957 (66.458%), random 942 (65.417%).
- Flip-exposure versus magnitude: -1.389 percentage points, graph-chip paired bootstrap 95% CI [-4.44pp,+1.60pp] — **no selection advantage**. Full sym versus raw: +3.75pp, cluster CI [-0.90pp,+8.54pp] — not established. Flip versus full sym: -4.17pp, cluster CI [-7.92pp,-0.55pp], exploratory.
- No-skew control (`sigma_skew=0`, 3 graphs×4 chips×30 restarts): raw, full sym and all partial corrections exactly tie at 233/360; digital exact 241/360 due to symmetric noise. Zero-noise 2-graph smoke reproduces exact digital decisions/outcomes across all arms. Skew-only control (`sigma_sym=0`): raw 252/360 versus digital/full sym 241/360, illustrating that nonreciprocity need not worsen finite solver success; small exploratory sample, not a claim of improvement.
- Source SHA-256 `7e2212935d193189fb499c773c19e4b057c668d6b6abfc56608a16209563c9a1`; output SHA-256 `50af267602b6d4af8e004cf0fd671df6a8ef4b7aa7c2d667862fbaee60aa2371`.

**Decision:** Reject the proposed simple flip-exposure-weighted selective symmetrization as a core contribution. A fully measured paired operator makes trivial full symmetrization a strong cheap control; physical mechanism and cost remain unverified. E10A does not reject all possible Ising algorithms.

## E03T — Repeated CAL-only selection with matched sampling budget

- Real, hash-verified frozen E01 MNIST packet (SHA-256 `400f094606615d73bfd90a0f32f7ef5d2ab0825bac67de1f3727c703c49f704a`), 128 CAL, 256 DEV parents, first FC1 256-row block, 8 output channels, 8 activation planes, signed W4 coefficients. Synthetic IID fixed-cell perturbations `sigma=.20`; bounded legal integer decoder, no physical MRAM read evidence.
- Candidate pool: E01 original, CAR, zero-rate sorted, dead-packed, balanced and random. Budget admissibility uses *CAL* group read counts normalized to DEV parent count (prior E03S accidentally used DEV reads for budget admission). At 6000/8000/15000 normalized CAL reads, the same candidate sets are eligible as before.
- Monte Carlo baseline: 12 independent CAL chip assignments per trial, **40 independently redrawn CAL sets**, paired across all mappings. The 48 DEV chip assignments remain *fixed* across these 40 repetitions and are never used to train or select. This is one held-out DEV population, not 40 statistically independent confirmations.
- At 6000 reads, marginal and Hermite1 select zero-rate 40/40; MC12 selects zero-rate 27/40 and CAR 13/40. On this independent DEV draw, CAR=654374.4, zero=703381.1 MSE; paired chip bootstrap difference (zero minus CAR) CI [-30091.4,+127136.4] contains zero.
- At 8000 reads, marginal selects zero 40/40, Hermite1 selects original 40/40, MC12 selects original10/dead11/zero14/CAR5. On independent DEV draw, original=712924.5 versus zero=703381.1 MSE, the Hermite choice is worse by 1.36% point estimate, with chip bootstrap original-minus-zero CI [-109708.4,+133349.3]. **No covariance advantage.**
- At 15000 reads, both analytic methods select balanced 40/40, while MC12 chooses balanced18 or random22. Balanced 545604.6 MSE is better than random on this held-out point estimate; analytic selection has a credible candidate-pool value but NOT attributable uniquely to covariance, as marginal also makes it.
- Timing on this local code path: analytic ~0.159s/policy including CAL preparation, direct MC12 ~0.088s/policy on prepared arrays; exclude common LUT/characterization. These timings are NOT fully matched end-to-end; the attempted 40-seed MC20 run exceeded execution limit and is **not a result**. A separate corrected equal-time comparison is still needed before claiming efficiency.

**Decision:** Do not claim the first-order Hermite covariance score is a useful improvement over marginal scoring or MC12. E03T does not reject all possible coupled mapping/failure estimators, nor does it measure full inference failures.
